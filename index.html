        Crafted for Cunard

        Our Abbey Road Listening Lounge is an exciting collaboration between music industry heavyweights. Let us introduce you to the specialists behind the scenes, those whose expertise are shaping your listening experience in the Commodore Club.

        Cunard and our Musical Heritage

        At Cunard, we’re committed to working with exceptional talent. Our dedicated team want to bring you the very best, whether that’s by visiting music colleges to discover rising stars, or by tirelessly hosting auditions so that the finest artists can be found.

        Today, we’re proud that our ships’ ensemble of resident musicians, and our own Royal Court Theatre company, include many multi-talented individuals. Not only that, but we have relationships with notable names in the arts. We’ve collaborated with the Society of London Theatre and the Olivier Awards, Abbey Road Studios, the Tony Awards, and the London Symphony Orchestra. 

        Both through these partnerships and with our own notoriety, Cunard ships have played host to many famous names over almost two centuries of history. Whether as guests or as entertainers, world-class musicians have joined us on board and found great joy in sailing the world with Cunard.

        Many of these artists have a distinct connection not only to Cunard, but with the famous Abbey Road Studios. So, as you take time in our Listening Lounge, enjoying the unique soundtracks curated by Abbey Road, discover a little more about those who have sailed before you.

        Edward Elgar

        British composer and conductor Edward Elgar, best known for his Pomp and Circumstance Marches, has early links with Cunard. Elgar sailed with Cunard across the Atlantic regularly and was present at the launch of Lusitania in 1906. There is a known diary entry from 1911, belonging to his wife, Alice Elgar, that speaks of his safe passage on board Mauretania to New York. Later in his life, in 1931, Elgar opened what is now Abbey Road Studios – and, conducting the London Symphony Orchestra, was the first to record there.

        Elizabeth Taylor

        1950s and 60s film star Elizabeth Taylor sailed with Cunard many times, on ships including the original Queen Mary, Queen Elizabeth, and Queen Elizabeth 2 (QE2). Sometimes sailing with other Hollywood stars like Audrey Hepburn and Cary Grant, Taylor enjoyed the glamour of a luxury Cunard voyage. She is known to have recorded for the 1977 film, A Little Night Music, in Abbey Road Studios’ Studio Two, in a closed session.

        David Bowie

        With a fear of flying, David Bowie was a regular guest of the Cunard fleet, joining many Transatlantic Crossings on the iconic QE2. On one occasion it’s reported that Bowie appeared at dinner in his Ziggy Stardust outfit, causing much excitement among guests. He’s said to have written songs on board for his album Aladdin Sane, which he’d go on to record at Abbey Road Studios. Bowie also recorded Ziggy Stardust there, as well as hits like Let’s Dance with Nile Rodgers. 

        Rod Stewart

        The QE2 also saw Rod Stewart join on many occasions, sailing with friends and partners. Joining a list of legends, Stewart was a guest of Cunard Transatlantic Crossings in the 1970s and 80s – a fact that won’t be lost on fans of the British singer-songwriter, whose 1975 album was called Atlantic Crossing. Having been making music since the 60s, Stewart has recorded all over, including at Abbey Road Studios in 2019. Here, he recorded You’re In My Heart with the Royal Philharmonic Orchestra in Studio One.

        Gregory Porter

        Known for his smooth voice and soulful music, Gregory Porter joined us on board Queen Mary 2 in 2015 for a special jazz-themed voyage. He put on a spell-binding performance that became a voyage highlight for many guests. Porter recorded parts of his sixth studio album, All Rise, at Abbey Road Studios’ Studio One in 2019 – the same year that we recorded our own production show, Symphony, there.

        Ed Sheeran

        Pop superstar Ed Sheeran sailed on Queen Mary 2 in 2016 from New York to London, on what was to be a truly unique Transatlantic Crossing. Travelling with Benny Blanco, his producer, the pair set about recording several songs for his album Divide, in a temporary studio that was constructed on board. Work on the album continued after their return to the UK, culminating in a session at Abbey Road Studios, with a 50-piece orchestra.

        Hugh Jackman

        In 2017, Queen Mary 2 hosted the world’s first film premiere on a passenger ship, for The Greatest Showman. A fitting setting for a film about P. T. Barnum - the 19th century showman sailed with Cunard frequently. As the star, Hugh Jackman was among the talent at the premiere on board – a man known for his prowess in musical film. Indeed, just five years prior in 2012, Jackman recorded vocals for the Les Misérables soundtrack at Abbey Road Studios.

        Jamie Cullum

        Jazz icon Jamie Cullum joined Queen Victoria in April 2025 for a special Atlantic Islands voyage. The MOBO Award-winner enchanted guests with his dynamic performances on board. He’s recorded at Abbey Road Studios multiple times, including the remarkable The Pianoman at Christmas in 2020 in Studio Two.

        The Beatles

        The Beatles’ connection to Abbey Road Studios is widely known thanks to their eponymous 1969 album. While there is no known account of a member of the band sailing with Cunard, there is a story that links them with our ocean liners. The story of the Cunard Yanks. These were the young British men who worked in our crew, sailing between Liverpool and New York in the 1940s, 50s, and 60s. After sailing and experiencing the United States, the Cunard Yanks are thanked for introducing new fashion, musical culture, and even jukeboxes to Liverpool. All this is said to have influenced Merseybeat and the Beatles themselves. 

        Paying homage to this legendary band, and the iconic Abbey Road Studios, in 2025 we recreated the famous zebra crossing album cover with our own Cunard bellhops

        Abbey Road Studios

        The home of music making

        Abbey Road Studios’ story began in 1929, with the purchase of a nine-bedroom house in St. John’s Wood in the northwest of London. This was followed by a meticulous two-year renovation, to transform it into the world’s first purpose-built recording studio. It set the tone from the start, with a rousing opening ceremony on 12 November 1931 when composer Sir Edward Elgar and the London Symphony Orchestra caught the attention of the world with a vivacious performance of Land of Hope and Glory. The doors were open, welcoming icons of the classical, orchestral, big band, and jazz genres.

        Over the years, their largest space, Studio One, cemented a reputation as the go-to destination for film score recordings. This hub of music creation continues to be the home of unforgettable film scores like Star Wars, Raiders of the Lost Ark, all the way to the Marvel titles and Barbie. Gaming as well, with unique soundtracks to the likes of Final Fantasy and World of Warcraft.

        The world’s most iconic recording studio

        Abbey Road Studios is perhaps best known for a portfolio that has shaped popular culture for nearly a century. Studios Two and Three are the birthplace of genre-defining songs and albums that have shaped the generations. The Beatles, Pink Floyd, Kate Bush, Fela Kuti, Shirley Bassey, Oasis, and so many more have made music history with the soundtracks to our memories. 

        The legendary Studio Two became the birthday place of British rock ‘n’ roll in the late ‘50s with the emergence of Cliff Richard and The Shadows and Johnny Kidd and The Pirates. However, not before EMI engineer Alan Blumlein led the very first stereo recording there in 1932 and popular dance bands like The Joe Loss Orchestra made it their home through the 1940s.

        As bombs rained down on London during World War II, Abbey Road’s doors remained open, hosting sessions including American bandleader Glenn Miller’s final recordings in 1944. In the ‘60s, bells and whistles and raucous laughter echoed in Studio Two, as comedy records by the likes of Peter Sellers and Morecombe and Wise were captured. Then, it became the recording home of the Merseybeat scene with artists like Cilla Black, Gerry and the Pacemakers, The Swinging Blue Jeans, and Billy J Kramer & the Dakotas. 

        We can’t underplay the importance of The Beatles and their impact on the studio. With the help of producer George Martin and countless Abbey Road engineers, the group recorded nearly all their catalogue there between 1962 and 1970. And of course ‘Abbey Road’ was made the name of their final set of recordings, making it their musical home. The strict three-hour session times turned into all-nighters, the technical engineers’ formal white coats disappeared, and both the engineers and artists pushed experimentation and creativity to the limit.

        Abbey Road Studios continues to inspire and empower new talent, reinforcing its role as a cornerstone of global music and culture.

        Innovation is key

        Creativity, technology, and innovation have always been at the heart of Abbey Road. If you look around you right now, you’ll get a feel of the studios in person with the display of technical equipment, signage, and memorabilia (from sessions with Pink Floyd and The Beatles) adding to the ambience. In the 1950s, the studio’s REDD was introduced – the Record Engineering Development Department. Here on board, you’ll see a REDD microphone and a special edition amplifier which incorporate original circuitry made by the REDD team, reimagined by Chandler Limited.

        Where Music Begins

        Bowers & Wilkins is revered and respected by the professionals who know sound best, from Oscar-winning musicians to the most famous recording studios in the world, including Abbey Road. At Bowers & Wilkins, we want listeners to feel inspired by the sounds we can create. We want them to feel connected to music – emotionally moved by it. For 60 years, our mission has been to find more ways for people to experience music and movies the way we experience it, with nothing added and nothing taken away.

        Bowers & Wilkins’ relationship with Abbey Road began in 1980 when the first 800 Series loudspeaker was adopted as the studio’s monitor of choice. It wasn't designed as a studio monitor, but when it was first used at the studios, the engineers at Abbey Road immediately understood its value.

Its highly revealing, ultra-low-distortion drive units are housed in uniquely designed enclosures that minimize unwanted resonances and ensure the most accurate reproduction of audio possible.
